💥 Damage & Fracture Solvers

FFTjax implements variational phase-field (AT2) fracture (solvers/damage/) as a staggered solve between the mechanical equilibrium problem (see Mechanical Solvers) and a Helmholtz-type damage sub-problem, both solved by matrix-free CG in Fourier space.

Variational formulation​

The solver is based on an energy minimization principle. For elasticity coupled to phase-field fracture, the total energy functional reads

Π(u,d)=∫Ωg(d) ψe(ε(u)) dΩ+∫ΩGc(d22ℓ+ℓ2∣∇d∣2)dΩ\Pi(u, d) = \int_\Omega g(d)\,\psi_e(\varepsilon(u)) \, d\Omega + \int_\Omega G_c \left( \frac{d^2}{2\ell} + \frac{\ell}{2} |\nabla d|^2 \right) d\OmegaΠ(u,d)=∫Ω​g(d)ψe​(ε(u))dΩ+∫Ω​Gc​(2ℓd2​+2ℓ​∣∇d∣2)dΩ

where

  • uuu is the displacement field
  • ddd is the phase-field variable (d=0d=0d=0 intact, d=1d=1d=1 fully broken)
  • g(d)=(1−k)(1−d)2+kg(d) = (1-k)(1-d)^2 + kg(d)=(1−k)(1−d)2+k is the quadratic degradation function, with a small residual stiffness k∼10−6k \sim 10^{-6}k∼10−6 kept for numerical conditioning rather than g(1)=0g(1)=0g(1)=0 exactly

Staggered solution scheme​

A variational staggered scheme alternates between the two Euler-Lagrange sub-problems of Π(u,d)\Pi(u,d)Π(u,d):

  1. Elastic step — minimize Π(u,dn)\Pi(u, d^{n})Π(u,dn) with respect to uuu at fixed damage, i.e. solve the mechanical equilibrium problem with the degraded stiffness g(dn) Cg(d^n)\,\mathbb{C}g(dn)C using the Newton-CG solvers described in Mechanical Solvers:
Au u=buA_u \, u = b_uAu​u=bu​
  1. Phase-field step — minimize Π(un+1,d)\Pi(u^{n+1}, d)Π(un+1,d) with respect to ddd at fixed displacement. This is a Helmholtz-type equation, solved by preconditioned CG entirely in Fourier space (solve_helmholtz_cg for homogeneous GcG_cGc​, solve_helmholtz_cg_het for spatially varying GcG_cGc​):
Ad d=bdA_d \, d = b_dAd​d=bd​

Both AuA_uAu​ and AdA_dAd​ are matrix-free spectral operators, so each sub-problem reuses the same FFT-based CG machinery as the mechanical solvers rather than needing a separate discretization. The two fields are updated alternately, dn→un+1→dn+1→…d^n \to u^{n+1} \to d^{n+1} \to \dotsdn→un+1→dn+1→…, until the coupled system converges.

Accelerating the staggered fixed point​

Plain (Picard) staggering is a fixed-point iteration xk+1=G(xk)x_{k+1} = G(x_k)xk+1​=G(xk​) that converges slowly — and can stall — near the brittle snap-through, where the elastic and damage sub-problems become strongly coupled. solvers/damage/anderson.py provides two drop-in accelerators that reuse the same (xk,G(xk))(x_k, G(x_k))(xk​,G(xk​)) pairs the staggered loop already produces:

  • Anderson mixing (Walker & Ni 2011, Type-II) solves an unconstrained least-squares problem on windowed differences of the residual fk=G(xk)−xkf_k = G(x_k)-x_kfk​=G(xk​)−xk​, then mixes the next iterate as xk+1=xk+βfk−(ΔX+βΔF)γx_{k+1} = x_k + \beta f_k - (\Delta X + \beta\Delta F)\gammaxk+1​=xk​+βfk​−(ΔX+βΔF)γ, damped by β∈(0,1]\beta \in (0,1]β∈(0,1] and Tikhonov-regularized against near-collinear residuals late in the snap-through.
  • NGMRES (Nonlinear GMRES) instead solves a constrained least-squares problem directly on the residuals, α=arg⁡min⁡∥Fα∥2\alpha = \arg\min\lVert F\alpha\rVert^2α=argmin∥Fα∥2 subject to 1⊤α=1\mathbf{1}^\top\alpha=11⊤α=1, and combines past map values as xk+1=Gαx_{k+1} = G\alphaxk+1​=Gα. This matches DAMASK's outer -snes_type ngmres solver (Schneider & Kästner 2025, doi:10.1111/ffe.14553); the sum-to-1 constraint stabilizes the solve when residuals become nearly collinear near snap-through and guarantees exact recovery of any fixed point x∗=G(x∗)x^* = G(x^*)x∗=G(x∗).

Both accelerators cost one staggered sweep per outer iteration — identical to plain Picard — with no extra function evaluations, and share the same reset()/step() API so either can be dropped into the staggered loop interchangeably.
