Damage & Fracture Solvers

FFTjax implements variational phase-field (AT2) fracture as a staggered solve between the mechanical equilibrium problem (see Mechanical Solvers) and a Helmholtz-type damage sub-problem, both solved by matrix-free CG in Fourier space. problems/fracture.py owns both the wiring (materials → per-voxel fields) and the staggered loop itself — kept out of solvers/, which stays numerics-only with no knowledge of "which degradation law" or "which energy split".

Variational formulation​

Π(u,d)=∫Ωg(d) ψe(ε(u)) dΩ+∫ΩGc(d22ℓ+ℓ2∣∇d∣2) dΩ\Pi(u,d) = \int_\Omega g(d)\,\psi_e(\varepsilon(u))\,d\Omega + \int_\Omega G_c\Big(\frac{d^2}{2\ell} + \frac{\ell}{2}|\nabla d|^2\Big)\,d\OmegaΠ(u,d)=∫Ω​g(d)ψe​(ε(u))dΩ+∫Ω​Gc​(2ℓd2​+2ℓ​∣∇d∣2)dΩ

where d∈[0,1]d\in[0,1]d∈[0,1] is the phase-field damage variable, g(d)=(1−k)(1−d)2+kg(d) = (1-k)(1-d)^2 + kg(d)=(1−k)(1−d)2+k is the quadratic AT2 degradation function with a small residual stiffness k∼10−6k\sim10^{-6}k∼10−6 (k_res, per-material) for numerical conditioning, GcG_cGc​ is the critical energy release rate (may be homogeneous or spatially heterogeneous), and ℓ\ellℓ is the length scale. Only the tensile part ψe+\psi_e^+ψe+​ of the strain energy drives damage growth (Amor et al. 2009 volumetric-deviatoric split, materialmodels/phasefield/splits.py):

ψ+=12K⟨tr ε⟩+2+μ εdev ⁣: ⁣εdev,ψ−=12K⟨tr ε⟩−2,K=λ+23μ\psi^+ = \tfrac12 K\langle\mathrm{tr}\,\varepsilon\rangle_+^2 + \mu\,\varepsilon_{\text{dev}}\!:\!\varepsilon_{\text{dev}}, \qquad \psi^- = \tfrac12 K\langle\mathrm{tr}\,\varepsilon\rangle_-^2, \qquad K=\lambda+\tfrac23\muψ+=21​K⟨trε⟩+2​+μεdev​:εdev​,ψ−=21​K⟨trε⟩−2​,K=λ+32​μ

All deviatoric energy is assigned to ψ+\psi^+ψ+ — a crack shouldn't heal or grow under compression, so only the volumetric part distinguishes tension from compression; ψ++ψ−\psi^++\psi^-ψ++ψ− reproduces the ordinary elastic energy exactly.

Two ways to apply degradation. degrade_stiffness_field (materialmodels/phasefield/ degradation.py) applies g(d)g(d)g(d) uniformly to an already-assembled stiffness field, Ceff=g(d)C\mathbb{C}_{\text{eff}} = g(d)\mathbb{C}Ceff​=g(d)C — simple, but this does not reproduce the Amor split at the stress level, since the compressive volumetric branch gets degraded too. PhaseFieldIsotropic (materialmodels/phasefield/isotropic.py) instead derives stress and tangent by automatic differentiation of one energy density, ψ(ε,d)=g(d) ψ+(ε)+ψ−(ε)\psi(\varepsilon,d) = g(d)\,\psi^+(\varepsilon) + \psi^-(\varepsilon)ψ(ε,d)=g(d)ψ+(ε)+ψ−(ε) — σ=∂ψ/∂ε\sigma = \partial\psi/\partial\varepsilonσ=∂ψ/∂ε (jax.grad), Ctan=∂σ/∂ε\mathbb{C}_{\text{tan}} = \partial\sigma/\partial\varepsilonCtan​=∂σ/∂ε (jax.jacfwd on that) — so the compressive branch is structurally undegraded (it never sees g(d)g(d)g(d)) rather than depending on two independently maintained formulas staying in sync. materialmodels.assembly.assemble_pff_local_update picks whichever a material provides (duck-typed on a psi_split attribute), so a materials list can freely mix both kinds.

Crack irreversibility is enforced through a monotone history variable. The hybrid formulation (Steinke & Kaliske 2019, adopted by Schneider & Kästner 2025, doi:10.1111/ffe.14553) only locks in monotonicity once a point has effectively cracked (d≥dthresd\ge d_{\text{thres}}d≥dthres​, default 0.95):

H={ψ+dprev<dthres(unrestricted)max⁡(Hprev,ψ+)dprev≥dthres(irreversible)H = \begin{cases} \psi^+ & d_{\text{prev}} < d_{\text{thres}} \quad\text{(unrestricted)}\\ \max(H_{\text{prev}}, \psi^+) & d_{\text{prev}} \ge d_{\text{thres}} \quad\text{(irreversible)} \end{cases}H={ψ+max(Hprev​,ψ+)​dprev​<dthres​(unrestricted)dprev​≥dthres​(irreversible)​

so the pre-crack process zone can still relax instead of being over-widened by premature history locking.

Staggered solution scheme​

problems.fracture.solve_fracture alternates the two Euler-Lagrange sub-problems of Π(u,d)\Pi(u,d)Π(u,d) until the coupled system converges (max⁡∣dnew−dold∣\max|d_{\text{new}}-d_{\text{old}}|max∣dnew​−dold​∣ below an absolute or relative tolerance):

  1. Elastic step — the degraded tangent Ceff\mathbb{C}_{\text{eff}}Ceff​ is evaluated at the previous staggered iteration's strain εprev\varepsilon_{\text{prev}}εprev​, not the current (still unknown) one — a frozen-tangent scheme. A material with a nonlinear-in-ε\varepsilonε degraded stress (PhaseFieldIsotropic's Amor split) would otherwise need its own nonlinear Newton-CG sub-solve at every staggered iteration; freezing the tangent keeps the mechanical step a plain linear CG solve (Lippmann-Schwinger, Fourier-Galerkin, or displacement-based — the same three formulations from Mechanical Solvers, including mixed strain/stress BC), and the loop still converges to the fully self-consistent solution at staggered convergence, where εprev=ε\varepsilon_{\text{prev}} = \varepsilonεprev​=ε.
  2. Phase-field step — a Helmholtz-type equation solved by preconditioned CG entirely in Fourier space (solvers/elliptic/scalar.py).

Homogeneous vs. heterogeneous damage solve​

Stationarity of Π\PiΠ with respect to ddd, using that −g′(d)=(1−k)(2−2d)-g'(d) = (1-k)(2-2d)−g′(d)=(1−k)(2−2d) is affine in ddd (what makes this a closed-form linear CG solve rather than a per-voxel Newton iteration):

Homogeneous GcG_cGc​ (solve_damage_helmholtz_cg) — a single scalar diffusion coefficient:

(Gcℓ+2(1−k)H)d−Gcℓ Δd=2(1−k)H\Big(\frac{G_c}{\ell} + 2(1-k)H\Big)d - G_c\ell\,\Delta d = 2(1-k)H(ℓGc​​+2(1−k)H)d−Gc​ℓΔd=2(1−k)H

Heterogeneous Gc(x)G_c(\mathbf{x})Gc​(x) (solve_damage_helmholtz_cg_het) — genuinely a different equation, not the same one at more generality: the variational derivative of the Gc(x)ℓ2∣∇d∣2G_c(\mathbf{x})\frac{\ell}{2}|\nabla d|^2Gc​(x)2ℓ​∣∇d∣2 term is ∇ ⁣⋅ ⁣(Gc(x)∇d)\nabla\!\cdot\!(G_c(\mathbf{x})\nabla d)∇⋅(Gc​(x)∇d), not Gc(x)ΔdG_c(\mathbf{x})\Delta dGc​(x)Δd (product rule — an extra ∇Gc⋅∇d\nabla G_c\cdot\nabla d∇Gc​⋅∇d term wherever GcG_cGc​ varies, including at a sharp phase interface):

(Gc(x)ℓ+2(1−k)H)d−ℓ ∇ ⁣⋅ ⁣(Gc(x)∇d)=2(1−k)H\Big(\frac{G_c(\mathbf{x})}{\ell} + 2(1-k)H\Big)d - \ell\,\nabla\!\cdot\!(G_c(\mathbf{x})\nabla d) = 2(1-k)H(ℓGc​(x)​+2(1−k)H)d−ℓ∇⋅(Gc​(x)∇d)=2(1−k)H

Plugging a per-voxel Gc(x)G_c(\mathbf{x})Gc​(x) pointwise into the homogeneous operator's GcΔdG_c\Delta dGc​Δd term would silently solve the wrong equation. The heterogeneous solve costs 3 FFT round trips per CG matvec (gradient, real-space multiply, divergence) instead of 1, and needs nyquist_safe_xi (its gradient/divergence are odd powers of ξ\boldsymbol\xiξ, unlike the homogeneous operator's even-power Laplacian). problems.fracture.solve_fracture dispatches between the two automatically — a per-voxel GcG_cGc​ that happens to be uniform collapses to the cheaper homogeneous solver.

An optional viscous term η/Δt\eta/\Delta tη/Δt adds resistance to rapid damage growth: without it, damage snap-through is instantaneous and spatially diffuse; with η>0\eta>0η>0 the crack grows gradually from the notch tip. Irreversibility is enforced post-solve, d=max⁡(dprev,dCG)d = \max(d_{\text{prev}}, d_{\text{CG}})d=max(dprev​,dCG​), and at kres=1k_{\text{res}}=1kres​=1 (a damage-immune phase) both driving terms vanish exactly, matching g′(d)≡0g'(d)\equiv0g′(d)≡0.

Accelerating the staggered fixed point​

Plain (Picard) staggering is a fixed-point iteration xk+1=G(xk)x_{k+1} = G(x_k)xk+1​=G(xk​) that converges slowly — and can stall — near the brittle snap-through, where the elastic and damage sub-problems become strongly coupled. solvers/damage/anderson.py provides two drop-in accelerators that reuse the same (xk,G(xk))(x_k, G(x_k))(xk​,G(xk​)) pairs the staggered loop already produces:

  • Anderson mixing (Walker & Ni 2011, Type-II) solves an unconstrained least-squares problem on windowed differences of the residual fk=G(xk)−xkf_k = G(x_k)-x_kfk​=G(xk​)−xk​, then mixes the next iterate as xk+1=xk+βfk−(ΔX+βΔF)γx_{k+1} = x_k + \beta f_k - (\Delta X + \beta\Delta F)\gammaxk+1​=xk​+βfk​−(ΔX+βΔF)γ, damped by β∈(0,1]\beta \in (0,1]β∈(0,1] and Tikhonov-regularized against near-collinear residuals late in the snap-through.
  • NGMRES (Nonlinear GMRES) instead solves a constrained least-squares problem directly on the residuals, α=arg⁡min⁡∥Fα∥2\alpha = \arg\min\lVert F\alpha\rVert^2α=argmin∥Fα∥2 subject to 1⊤α=1\mathbf{1}^\top\alpha=11⊤α=1, and combines past map values as xk+1=Gαx_{k+1} = G\alphaxk+1​=Gα. This matches DAMASK's outer -snes_type ngmres solver (Schneider & Kästner 2025, doi:10.1111/ffe.14553); the sum-to-1 constraint stabilizes the solve when residuals become nearly collinear near snap-through and guarantees exact recovery of any fixed point x∗=G(x∗)x^* = G(x^*)x∗=G(x∗).

Both accelerators cost one staggered sweep per outer iteration — identical to plain Picard — with no extra function evaluations, and share the same reset()/step() API so either can be dropped into the staggered loop interchangeably. problems/fracture.py selects one via the accelerator: anderson | ngmres | none config key (none is the default and is bit-for-bit the un-accelerated loop; depth 1 is also exactly Picard).

What they buy, measured​

On a linear fixed point with a known solution, sweeps to a 10−1010^{-10}10−10 residual — note the speedup grows with difficulty, which is what matters, since a staggered loop is cheap when it is easy (2 sweeps) and ruinous when it is hard (44):

spectral radiusPicardAnderson (depth 5)NGMRES (depth 5)
0.97610130 (4.7×)130 (4.7×)
0.9953699600 (6.2×)600 (6.2×)

Through the real coupled solver, one increment pre-severance: Picard 11 sweeps, either accelerator 4 sweeps (2.75×), reaching the same state to 6×10−56\times10^{-5}6×10−5 MPa in stress and 2.8×10−62.8\times10^{-6}2.8×10−6 in damage — a shorter path to the same fixed point.

Three details that are not in the textbook version​

  • Clamping. The iterate is a damage field, and an extrapolated step is a linear combination with possibly negative weights, so it can leave [0,1][0,1][0,1] or move damage backwards. Every iterate is clamped to [0,1][0,1][0,1] and to an irreversibility floor (the increment's starting damage). Without the floor, acceleration can un-crack voxels.
  • The safeguard is retrospective. Anderson has no descent guarantee, so an accelerated step is judged by the residual the next sweep actually produces: if ∥fk+1∥>safeguard_factor ∥fk∥\lVert f_{k+1}\rVert > \texttt{safeguard\_factor}\,\lVert f_k\rVert∥fk+1​∥>safeguard_factor∥fk​∥ the history is flushed and the step falls back to Picard. This costs nothing, since that sweep happens anyway. A prospective test on step length is wrong — an accelerated step is supposed to travel further than the Picard step, and such a rule rejects precisely the steps that work (measured: 497 rejections in 499 steps, zero speedup).
  • Convergence is measured on the Picard residual ∥G(d)−d∥\lVert G(d)-d\rVert∥G(d)−d∥, not on the accelerated step ∥dk+1−dk∥\lVert d_{k+1}-d_k\rVert∥dk+1​−dk​∥. They coincide without an accelerator, but an extrapolated step is deliberately larger, so testing the step would make a converging run look divergent.
Reset once per load increment

The fixed point moves with the applied strain, so history from the previous increment extrapolates toward the previous solution. problems/fracture.py calls reset(floor=d_init) at the start of every increment.

Fracture of an elastoplastic matrix​

The formulation above assumes the bulk is elastic up to failure. A polymer matrix is not: it yields — in a markedly pressure-sensitive way — well before it cracks, so a brittle matrix over-predicts its strength and mis-places the crack. materialmodels/phasefield/drucker_prager.py couples AT2 damage to the Drucker-Prager elastoplastic model of Mechanical Solvers through the effective stress.

Effective-stress coupling​

The plastic return mapping runs in undegraded stress space, exactly as it does without damage, and ddd then scales what the material transmits:

σeff=C:(ε−εp),σ=g(d)(seff+⟨peff⟩+I)+⟨peff⟩−I\boldsymbol\sigma_{\text{eff}} = \mathbb{C} : (\boldsymbol\varepsilon - \boldsymbol\varepsilon_p), \qquad \boldsymbol\sigma = g(d)\left(\mathbf{s}_{\text{eff}} + \langle p_{\text{eff}}\rangle_+\mathbf{I}\right) + \langle p_{\text{eff}}\rangle_-\mathbf{I}σeff​=C:(ε−εp​),σ=g(d)(seff​+⟨peff​⟩+​I)+⟨peff​⟩−​I

with seff\mathbf{s}_{\text{eff}}seff​ and peffp_{\text{eff}}peff​ the deviatoric and mean parts of σeff\boldsymbol\sigma_{\text{eff}}σeff​. This is Amor's split applied at the stress level: everything degrades except the compressive hydrostatic branch, so a fully cracked voxel still carries compression rather than interpenetrating. The yield surface never sees ddd, which is what lets the return mapping — cone, apex, tip-rounded and all of their guards — be reused unchanged.

Degrading the yield stress as well (σy→g(d)σy\sigma_y \to g(d)\sigma_yσy​→g(d)σy​, so a cracking voxel also loses strength) is the fuller coupling and is not implemented.

Driving force​

H=ψe+(ε−εp)+βp Wp,Wp=∫σeff:dεp\mathcal{H} = \psi_e^+(\boldsymbol\varepsilon - \boldsymbol\varepsilon_p) + \beta_p \, W_p , \qquad W_p = \int \boldsymbol\sigma_{\text{eff}} : \mathrm{d}\boldsymbol\varepsilon_pH=ψe+​(ε−εp​)+βp​Wp​,Wp​=∫σeff​:dεp​

Two parts, with βp\beta_pβp​ selecting the physics:

  • βp=0\beta_p = 0βp​=0 — a brittle crack in a plastic solid. Plasticity affects fracture only by capping the elastic energy that can be stored, which blunts the crack. Note ψe+\psi_e^+ψe+​ is evaluated on the elastic strain: plastic strain stores no energy, and using the total strain would let plastic flow drive cracking through the back door.
  • βp>0\beta_p > 0βp​>0 — ductile fracture: accumulated plastic work also drives the crack, so a voxel that has yielded heavily cracks sooner. βp\beta_pβp​ trades off directly against GcG_cGc​, so re-calibrate GcG_cGc​ if you change it.

WpW_pWp​ is accumulated as σeff:dεp\boldsymbol\sigma_{\text{eff}}:\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol\varepsilon_pσeff​:dεp​, which is exact for associated and non-associated flow — the closed form ∫σy dα\int\sigma_y\,\mathrm{d}\alpha∫σy​dα is not, since σ:dεp=dλ (σy+3(ag−af)p)\boldsymbol\sigma:\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol\varepsilon_p = \mathrm{d}\lambda\,(\sigma_y + 3(a_g-a_f)p)σ:dεp​=dλ(σy​+3(ag​−af​)p) and the two agree only at ag=afa_g = a_fag​=af​.

H\mathcal{H}H enters the damage sub-problem exactly where ψ+\psi^+ψ+ did, so the Helmholtz solve is structurally unchanged.

What this costs numerically​

The mechanical sub-problem is now genuinely nonlinear and path-dependent, so the staggered loop switches from one linear CG solve to a Newton iteration (solve_displacement_based_nonlinear); problems/fracture.py selects this automatically when a coupled material is present, which also means formulation: displacement is required and the run can no longer be vmapped over several RVE realizations.

Two consequences worth knowing before trusting a result:

  • The tangent is non-symmetric wherever a voxel is both damaged and plastic, and increasingly so with ddd. For associated flow, where the Drucker-Prager tangent alone is exactly symmetric, the relative major asymmetry ∣Cijkl−Cklij∣/∣C∣|C_{ijkl}-C_{klij}|/|C|∣Cijkl​−Cklij​∣/∣C∣ measures 3×10−163\times10^{-16}3×10−16, 5×10−25\times10^{-2}5×10−2, 2×10−12\times10^{-1}2×10−1 and 3×10−13\times10^{-1}3×10−1 at d=0, 0.1, 0.5, 0.9d = 0,\,0.1,\,0.5,\,0.9d=0,0.1,0.5,0.9, while a purely elastic state stays symmetric to 10−1610^{-16}10−16 at any ddd. The Amor operator gP++P−g\mathbf{P}_+ + \mathbf{P}_-gP+​+P−​ commutes with the isotropic elastic tangent but not with the elastoplastic one's s⊗s\mathbf{s}\otimes\mathbf{s}s⊗s structure. CG is applied to it unsymmetrized, which is formally outside CG's assumptions.

  • Results past the softening branch are unverified. Newton converges cleanly through the load, the peak and the softening. Once the transmitted stress is three to four orders below peak it stops converging and can return a small negative macroscopic stress under applied tension. The cause is not established — severance, the ∼106\sim10^6∼106 stiffness contrast, the preconditioner choice and plastic-strain-driven compressive strutting were each tested and ruled out. The mechanical failure is folded into the increment's convergence flag, so the load stepper in automatic mode cuts back on it rather than accepting a non-equilibrium state.

  • Plastic strain inside a crack is not meaningful. A fully cracked voxel keeps its effective stress on the yield surface, so εp\boldsymbol\varepsilon_pεp​ and α\alphaα go on growing while it transmits almost nothing. This is inherent to effective-stress ductile phase-field; mask on damage before homogenizing a plastic strain.
