Damage & Fracture Solvers

FFTjax implements variational phase-field (AT2) fracture as a staggered solve between the mechanical equilibrium problem (see Mechanical Solvers) and a Helmholtz-type damage sub-problem, both solved by matrix-free CG in Fourier space. problems/fracture.py owns both the wiring (materials → per-voxel fields) and the staggered loop itself — kept out of solvers/, which stays numerics-only with no knowledge of "which degradation law" or "which energy split".

Variational formulation​

Π(u,d)=∫Ωg(d) ψe(ε(u)) dΩ+∫ΩGc(d22ℓ+ℓ2∣∇d∣2) dΩ\Pi(u,d) = \int_\Omega g(d)\,\psi_e(\varepsilon(u))\,d\Omega + \int_\Omega G_c\Big(\frac{d^2}{2\ell} + \frac{\ell}{2}|\nabla d|^2\Big)\,d\OmegaΠ(u,d)=∫Ω​g(d)ψe​(ε(u))dΩ+∫Ω​Gc​(2ℓd2​+2ℓ​∣∇d∣2)dΩ

where d∈[0,1]d\in[0,1]d∈[0,1] is the phase-field damage variable, g(d)=(1−k)(1−d)2+kg(d) = (1-k)(1-d)^2 + kg(d)=(1−k)(1−d)2+k is the quadratic AT2 degradation function with a small residual stiffness k∼10−6k\sim10^{-6}k∼10−6 (k_res, per-material) for numerical conditioning, GcG_cGc​ is the critical energy release rate (may be homogeneous or spatially heterogeneous), and ℓ\ellℓ is the length scale. Only the tensile part ψe+\psi_e^+ψe+​ of the strain energy drives damage growth (Amor et al. 2009 volumetric-deviatoric split, materialmodels/phasefield/splits.py):

ψ+=12K⟨tr ε⟩+2+μ εdev ⁣: ⁣εdev,ψ−=12K⟨tr ε⟩−2,K=λ+23μ\psi^+ = \tfrac12 K\langle\mathrm{tr}\,\varepsilon\rangle_+^2 + \mu\,\varepsilon_{\text{dev}}\!:\!\varepsilon_{\text{dev}}, \qquad \psi^- = \tfrac12 K\langle\mathrm{tr}\,\varepsilon\rangle_-^2, \qquad K=\lambda+\tfrac23\muψ+=21​K⟨trε⟩+2​+μεdev​:εdev​,ψ−=21​K⟨trε⟩−2​,K=λ+32​μ

All deviatoric energy is assigned to ψ+\psi^+ψ+ — a crack shouldn't heal or grow under compression, so only the volumetric part distinguishes tension from compression; ψ++ψ−\psi^++\psi^-ψ++ψ− reproduces the ordinary elastic energy exactly.

Two ways to apply degradation. degrade_stiffness_field (materialmodels/phasefield/ degradation.py) applies g(d)g(d)g(d) uniformly to an already-assembled stiffness field, Ceff=g(d)C\mathbb{C}_{\text{eff}} = g(d)\mathbb{C}Ceff​=g(d)C — simple, but this does not reproduce the Amor split at the stress level, since the compressive volumetric branch gets degraded too. PhaseFieldIsotropic (materialmodels/phasefield/isotropic.py) instead derives stress and tangent by automatic differentiation of one energy density, ψ(ε,d)=g(d) ψ+(ε)+ψ−(ε)\psi(\varepsilon,d) = g(d)\,\psi^+(\varepsilon) + \psi^-(\varepsilon)ψ(ε,d)=g(d)ψ+(ε)+ψ−(ε) — σ=∂ψ/∂ε\sigma = \partial\psi/\partial\varepsilonσ=∂ψ/∂ε (jax.grad), Ctan=∂σ/∂ε\mathbb{C}_{\text{tan}} = \partial\sigma/\partial\varepsilonCtan​=∂σ/∂ε (jax.jacfwd on that) — so the compressive branch is structurally undegraded (it never sees g(d)g(d)g(d)) rather than depending on two independently maintained formulas staying in sync. materialmodels.assembly.assemble_pff_local_update picks whichever a material provides (duck-typed on a psi_split attribute), so a materials list can freely mix both kinds.

Crack irreversibility is enforced through a monotone history variable. The hybrid formulation (Steinke & Kaliske 2019, adopted by Schneider & Kästner 2025, doi:10.1111/ffe.14553) only locks in monotonicity once a point has effectively cracked (d≥dthresd\ge d_{\text{thres}}d≥dthres​, default 0.95):

H={ψ+dprev<dthres(unrestricted)max⁡(Hprev,ψ+)dprev≥dthres(irreversible)H = \begin{cases} \psi^+ & d_{\text{prev}} < d_{\text{thres}} \quad\text{(unrestricted)}\\ \max(H_{\text{prev}}, \psi^+) & d_{\text{prev}} \ge d_{\text{thres}} \quad\text{(irreversible)} \end{cases}H={ψ+max(Hprev​,ψ+)​dprev​<dthres​(unrestricted)dprev​≥dthres​(irreversible)​

so the pre-crack process zone can still relax instead of being over-widened by premature history locking.

Staggered solution scheme​

problems.fracture.solve_fracture alternates the two Euler-Lagrange sub-problems of Π(u,d)\Pi(u,d)Π(u,d) until the coupled system converges (max⁡∣dnew−dold∣\max|d_{\text{new}}-d_{\text{old}}|max∣dnew​−dold​∣ below an absolute or relative tolerance):

  1. Elastic step — the degraded tangent Ceff\mathbb{C}_{\text{eff}}Ceff​ is evaluated at the previous staggered iteration's strain εprev\varepsilon_{\text{prev}}εprev​, not the current (still unknown) one — a frozen-tangent scheme. A material with a nonlinear-in-ε\varepsilonε degraded stress (PhaseFieldIsotropic's Amor split) would otherwise need its own nonlinear Newton-CG sub-solve at every staggered iteration; freezing the tangent keeps the mechanical step a plain linear CG solve (Lippmann-Schwinger, Fourier-Galerkin, or displacement-based — the same three formulations from Mechanical Solvers, including mixed strain/stress BC), and the loop still converges to the fully self-consistent solution at staggered convergence, where εprev=ε\varepsilon_{\text{prev}} = \varepsilonεprev​=ε.
  2. Phase-field step — a Helmholtz-type equation solved by preconditioned CG entirely in Fourier space (solvers/elliptic/scalar.py).

Homogeneous vs. heterogeneous damage solve​

Stationarity of Π\PiΠ with respect to ddd, using that −g′(d)=(1−k)(2−2d)-g'(d) = (1-k)(2-2d)−g′(d)=(1−k)(2−2d) is affine in ddd (what makes this a closed-form linear CG solve rather than a per-voxel Newton iteration):

Homogeneous GcG_cGc​ (solve_damage_helmholtz_cg) — a single scalar diffusion coefficient:

(Gcℓ+2(1−k)H)d−Gcℓ Δd=2(1−k)H\Big(\frac{G_c}{\ell} + 2(1-k)H\Big)d - G_c\ell\,\Delta d = 2(1-k)H(ℓGc​​+2(1−k)H)d−Gc​ℓΔd=2(1−k)H

Heterogeneous Gc(x)G_c(\mathbf{x})Gc​(x) (solve_damage_helmholtz_cg_het) — genuinely a different equation, not the same one at more generality: the variational derivative of the Gc(x)ℓ2∣∇d∣2G_c(\mathbf{x})\frac{\ell}{2}|\nabla d|^2Gc​(x)2ℓ​∣∇d∣2 term is ∇ ⁣⋅ ⁣(Gc(x)∇d)\nabla\!\cdot\!(G_c(\mathbf{x})\nabla d)∇⋅(Gc​(x)∇d), not Gc(x)ΔdG_c(\mathbf{x})\Delta dGc​(x)Δd (product rule — an extra ∇Gc⋅∇d\nabla G_c\cdot\nabla d∇Gc​⋅∇d term wherever GcG_cGc​ varies, including at a sharp phase interface):

(Gc(x)ℓ+2(1−k)H)d−ℓ ∇ ⁣⋅ ⁣(Gc(x)∇d)=2(1−k)H\Big(\frac{G_c(\mathbf{x})}{\ell} + 2(1-k)H\Big)d - \ell\,\nabla\!\cdot\!(G_c(\mathbf{x})\nabla d) = 2(1-k)H(ℓGc​(x)​+2(1−k)H)d−ℓ∇⋅(Gc​(x)∇d)=2(1−k)H

Plugging a per-voxel Gc(x)G_c(\mathbf{x})Gc​(x) pointwise into the homogeneous operator's GcΔdG_c\Delta dGc​Δd term would silently solve the wrong equation. The heterogeneous solve costs 3 FFT round trips per CG matvec (gradient, real-space multiply, divergence) instead of 1, and needs nyquist_safe_xi (its gradient/divergence are odd powers of ξ\boldsymbol\xiξ, unlike the homogeneous operator's even-power Laplacian). problems.fracture.solve_fracture dispatches between the two automatically — a per-voxel GcG_cGc​ that happens to be uniform collapses to the cheaper homogeneous solver.

An optional viscous term η/Δt\eta/\Delta tη/Δt adds resistance to rapid damage growth: without it, damage snap-through is instantaneous and spatially diffuse; with η>0\eta>0η>0 the crack grows gradually from the notch tip. Irreversibility is enforced post-solve, d=max⁡(dprev,dCG)d = \max(d_{\text{prev}}, d_{\text{CG}})d=max(dprev​,dCG​), and at kres=1k_{\text{res}}=1kres​=1 (a damage-immune phase) both driving terms vanish exactly, matching g′(d)≡0g'(d)\equiv0g′(d)≡0.
